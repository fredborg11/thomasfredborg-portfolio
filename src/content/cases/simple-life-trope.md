## The brief

Simple Life by Trope wanted a completely new and playful take on a range of snack bags covering banana chips, honey-roasted peanuts, spicy rice cakes, crunchy peanut balls, crunchy corn and potato sticks, and cheese-flavoured chips.

The products were developed as spot products for Rema 1000, so the packaging needed to be highly visible in-store and appeal to a younger audience. The idea was to create bags that were hard to ignore — the kind of products parents might find difficult to leave behind on a Friday afternoon.

## My role

I handled the graphic development of the entire series, working closely with the client on the product names and taking the designs from concept to print-ready artwork.

My role included:

- Developing product names together with the client
- Developing the overall visual expression
- Creating the illustrated characters and supporting drawings
- Selecting typography and colour palettes
- Defining the tone of voice
- Designing the six packaging variants
- Preparing and delivering final print-ready artwork

The client handled proofreading, sales, distribution and subsequent marketing.

## One series. Six personalities.

Once the product names were established, I used each one as the starting point for a character-driven visual concept.

**Gorilla Guf** became a playful gorilla holding a banana.

**Guldklumper** turned into a gold prospector searching for the next nugget.

**Kapow!** was built around an overheated rice-cake character reacting to the product's spicy kick.

**Ninja Kugler** naturally became a round ninja character.

**Trommestikker** evolved into a rock-star character to give the product a sense of energy and attitude.

**UFO'er** took its cue directly from the name and became a playful science-fiction universe built around a flying saucer.

The aim was to make each product instantly recognisable while keeping the whole range connected through a shared playful and energetic visual language.

## Colour, character and shelf impact

The colour palette was chosen with two things in mind: strong shelf impact and clear differentiation between the individual products.

Each variant received its own dominant colour and character, allowing the six bags to stand apart while still reading as one coherent collection.

The visual language was deliberately colourful, humorous and lively. The overall feeling I wanted to create was the anticipation of taking the snacks home for a fun Friday night in front of the TV or putting them on the table at a family gathering.

## From design to shelf

The final result was a fully produced six-product packaging series that launched as spot products in Rema 1000 stores across Denmark.

I supplied the finished design files ready for production. The client was responsible for the commercial launch, sales, distribution and marketing of the products.

I don't have access to sales figures for the series, so this case focuses on the design solution, illustration work and the final produced packaging rather than commercial performance.
