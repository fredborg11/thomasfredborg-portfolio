## The brief

In 2019, Fair Fitness came to the company I work for with a clear challenge: their centres had accumulated many different types of graphic material without a consistent visual line.

I became the main point of contact for the customer and have since helped streamline and further develop the visual identity used across their centres.

The goal has been straightforward: when a member moves from one Fair Fitness location to another, the environment should still feel familiar and recognisably Fair Fitness.

## My role

I handle the graphic production for Fair Fitness's centres from brief to delivery.

My work includes:

- Wall art
- Logo signage
- Light wall graphics
- Posters
- Infographics
- Centre signage
- Environmental graphics
- Toilets and changing-room communication
- Large-format print preparation
- Supplier coordination and delivery

I work directly with Fair Fitness's two centre managers, manage the graphic process independently and coordinate directly with the relevant production suppliers.

## Building consistency across 18 centres

This is not a campaign-driven project. The focus is on consistency, practical communication and making sure the same visual standards work across a growing network of locations.

Over time, I have helped create a clearer red thread across the centre environments by applying and further developing the visual identity across different formats and physical touchpoints.

The result is a more coherent experience across Fair Fitness's 18 centres on Zealand, even though each location is a different physical space.

## From brief to finished material

Most assignments start with a practical brief: a new centre is opening, an existing centre is being renovated, or new material is needed for a specific area.

I then develop or prepare the material, handle the corrections, create the final print files and manage the production process with suppliers.

For large-format wall art, much of the work is about preparing and correcting supplied imagery so it can work at scale. Other assignments require more layout and graphic development, depending on the application.

The customer can therefore hand over an assignment and trust that it is carried through the entire process.

## A long-term design partnership

After seven years, the collaboration is still active whenever Fair Fitness opens a new centre or renovates an existing one.

For me, the value of the project lies in the continuity: understanding the customer, knowing the visual system, keeping an overview of all the material and taking responsibility for the process from brief to finished delivery.

It is a good example of the kind of role I enjoy in design — not only creating the graphics, but also making the customer's life easier by taking ownership of the task all the way through.
